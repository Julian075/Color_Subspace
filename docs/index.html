Overview of Color Alignment and Applications. Across diverse modern diffusion architectures (FLUX, FLUX.2, SD3, SD3.5, SDXL, PixArt, Z-Image), VAE latent representations inherently organize chromaticity along a low-dimensional, orthogonal color subspace. By characterizing these latent directions, our closed-loop steering framework enables precise numerical color control (ColorTuning), multi-zone semantic color transfer from palettes or photographic references, and continuous spatially-adaptive gamut reduction directly at generation time without retraining.

Overview

Abstract

Variational autoencoders (VAEs) are a fundamental backbone of modern text-to-image models, which generate images within their latent space. VAEs are known to disentangle the main factors of variation in natural image statistics: decorrelating them yields one luminance axis and two opponent-color axes. Color should therefore be expected to emerge as a distinct factor in the VAE latent space. Yet how these latent spaces represent color remains largely unexplored. In this work, we show that the VAEs of text-to-image models share an intrinsic color subspace aligned with brightness and opponent colors. Through a linear approximation of the encoder and targeted latent steering, we recover this subspace consistently across a broad range of VAEs, from SD1.5 to FLUX.2 and Z-Image. Building on this characterization, we propose three generation-time applications: ColorTuning, which achieves state-of-the-art in precise numerical color generation on GenColorBench; Multi-Zone Semantic Color Transfer, to steer scene palettes to match reference swatches or photographs; and Spatially-Adaptive Gamut Reduction, to continuously modulate chromatic intensity without hue cancellation.
Foundations

Color in Autoencoder Latent Spaces

Traversing principal latent directions in pretrained VAEs reveals an opponent-color plane and brightness axis that emerge spontaneously without explicit color supervision.

FLUX.1-dev (16 latent channels): Perturbing leading latent directions spans an orthogonal opponent-color plane (yellow-blue vs. green-red) and independent luminance control while faithfully preserving high-frequency image structure.

FLUX.2-dev (32 latent channels): Even with higher dimensional latent spaces, singular vector analysis recovers the same canonical chromatic organization, demonstrating model-agnostic universality across generations.

Stable Diffusion 3 (16 latent channels): Leading latent components isolate opponent-color transitions across natural scenes without affecting spatial geometry.

SDXL (4 latent channels): In compact 4-channel latents, color information aligns directly with dominant channels, confirming that efficient image compression inherently isolates chromaticity.

Qualitative Results

Further Color Applications During Generation

Leveraging the discovered color subspace enables diverse generation-time applications using the exact same underlying representation.

Application 1

Precise Numerical Color Tuning

Closed-loop trajectory steering during denoising. Converts numerical color codes into proxy color names for prompt text conditioning, and applies residual ResMLP displacements inside SAM3 object masks to achieve exact CIELAB targets (ΔE00 < 5.0).

Application 2

Multi-Zone Semantic Color Transfer

Extracts dominant and focal color clusters from reference palette swatches or photographs. Segments the scene into semantic zones and applies independent latent shifts to match the reference distribution without color bleeding.

Application 3

Spatially-Adaptive Gamut Reduction

Modulates image saturation toward narrower color gamuts pixel-by-pixel. Contracts chroma coordinates (a*, b*) toward neutral gray while preserving lightness (L*), eliminating the complementary hue cancellations of uniform shifts.
